Manual unit curation

Every sorter produces candidate clusters. Manual review records the evidence used to retain, reject, or defer each candidate without erasing the native sorter output.

Why curation remains necessary

Kilosort4, MountainSort5, SpyKING CIRCUS 2, and other sorters use different detection and clustering strategies. Their unit counts can differ, and agreement measures reproducibility rather than biological truth. Curation applies an explicit laboratory decision rule to the evidence.

Review procedure

  1. Open Unit QC after activating the sorter result that will be reviewed.
  2. Select a unit. Inspect waveform, autocorrelogram, ISI violations, firing rate, SNR, amplitude over time, and available sorter diagnostics.
  3. Check waveform consistency across time and channels. Abrupt discontinuity or implausible shape requires closer review.
  4. Check the refractory-period evidence. A single threshold never settles identity by itself.
  5. Inspect amplitude and firing-rate stability across the whole recording; short fragments can hide drift or recording loss.
  6. Compare nearby or highly similar clusters for possible duplication, split, or merge errors.
  7. Choose a label, complete the evidence checklist, write an expert note, and save the decision.
  8. Repeat for every retained candidate. Export the decision table with reviewer, timestamp, metric snapshot, sorter, and source run.

Available labels

LabelUse
Candidate single unitWaveform, refractory period, stability, and isolation support single-unit use; final scientific inclusion still follows the study protocol.
Multi-unit activityBiological spiking is present, while separation from nearby units remains insufficient.
NoiseThe cluster is dominated by artifacts, electrical noise, or invalid waveform structure.
UncertainEvidence is incomplete or conflicting. The unit remains available for later review.

Sorter-specific and shared evidence

The checklist and labels are sorter-independent. Native diagnostics remain attached to each result: Kilosort templates, amplitudes, depth-time plots, drift and contamination fields stay available; other sorters retain their own native files. Advanced split/merge work can continue in Phy or another dedicated curator and return through the sorting-result adapter.
