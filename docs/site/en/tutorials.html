01

Start with the data structure

This page does more than select a file. NeuroEphys AI must know how each sample is stored, how many values form one time point, how probe contacts are arranged, and which device clock timestamps behavior. Those facts give filtering, sorting, and event alignment their correct physical units. Import keeps the source read-only and stores only links, caches, and derived results in the project.

Before you begin

Have the acquisition notes, sampling rate, total channel count, dtype, gain or µV/bit, probe geometry, and optional event CSV ready. For a generic binary file, copy values from the acquisition configuration rather than guessing from the traces.

Operations and purpose

Create an empty projectChoose a project name and location, then import data inside it.Separates source data from analysis products and creates a restore point.Creates neuroflow_project.json plus derived, results, and exports folders.
Import generic binarySelect .bin/.dat/.raw and enter rate, channels, dtype, and µV/bit.Reads interleaved recordings without self-describing metadata.Validates file size against channels × bytes per sample and displays traces.
Import acquisition-system dataChoose Intan, Open Ephys, SpikeGLX, Blackrock, Plexon, TDT, or NWB.Uses a SpikeInterface extractor to read metadata and build a common cache.Stores a normalized int16 cache while leaving source files unchanged.
Resume from existing sortingSelect Kilosort/Phy, IBL ALF, or an NWB file with Units.Skips unavailable raw stages and continues with units and downstream analyses.Produces normalized spike times in seconds, unit IDs, and provenance.

Recommended sequence

  1. Create a project first, then choose the entry that matches the files you actually have.
  2. Inspect 50–100 ms of traces and verify channel count, amplitude units, and duration.
  3. Save the project; reopening neuroflow_project.json should restore the page and workflow state.

Common mistakes

  • Using 384 ephys channels as the file total when a sync channel is also stored.
  • Moving an external source and expecting a read-only link to discover its new location.
  • Expecting IBL ALF or Units-only NWB to provide raw voltage for re-sorting.

Inputs, outputs, and acceptance check

Input：Raw acquisition files, probe information, and event logs

Output：Read-only source links and a NeuroEphys AI project manifest

Check：Check file size, rate, channel count, dtype, and whether events use seconds, milliseconds, or samples. Inspect traces immediately after import.

Next： After confirming structure, continue to Raw QC; processed spikes begin at Unit QC.

02

Raw-signal quality control

QC does not automatically delete channels. It builds evidence about where an anomaly occurs in channel, time, and frequency and whether it can affect referencing or spike detection. Locate problems globally, verify them in zoomed traces, and only then label candidate bad channels.

Before you begin

Verify sampling rate, channel count, units, and duration on the Data page.

Operations and purpose

OperationWhat you doPurposeResult
Multichannel raw tracesBrowse with time window, start time, channel range, and display gain.Find clipping, jumps, motion artifacts, dead channels, and common noise.Produces zoomable voltage snippets and RMS for the current window.
QC metric summaryRun the sub-analysis and inspect RMS, peak-to-peak, clipping, and line-noise ratios.Screens channels using several metrics rather than one threshold.Creates a candidate bad-channel table without automatic exclusion.
Channel-by-frequency powerInspect the channel-by-frequency power map and zoom suspicious bands.Distinguishes broadband noise, 50/60 Hz line noise, and local channel problems.Produces channel spectra and line-frequency evidence.
Quality timelineCompute amplitude, noise, and clipping metrics in time blocks.Finds dropouts, drift, or artifacts limited to part of the session.Produces suspect intervals for exclusion or repair during preprocessing.

Parameters

ParameterMeaningDefaultRecommendationEffect of changing it
Time startStart of the displayed recording window.0 sInspect the beginning, middle, end, and intervals around key task events.Changes the view and local metrics without cropping the source.
WindowDuration displayed at once.60 msUse 20–100 ms for spikes and 1–10 s for slow artifacts or rhythms.Short windows reveal waveforms; long windows reveal trends but compress spikes.
Channel rangeFirst channel and number shown together.Ch 0–11Inspect 8–16 channels at a time on dense probes and move along depth.Too many channels reduce readability; too few can hide spatially shared patterns.

Every page control

Channel RMS
Click a bar to inspect channel number and RMS; flagged channels use the warning color.

Bad-channel candidates

Recommended sequence

  1. Run summary and timeline first, then verify every candidate in traces and spectra.
  2. Record evidence and the manual decision; do not delete channels directly on the QC page.
  3. Save QC decisions before preprocessing so bad channels do not contaminate referencing.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing a high-firing channel with a noisy channel.
  • Declaring the whole session good after inspecting only the first milliseconds.
  • Applying a notch immediately without checking grounding, reference, and harmonics.

Inputs, outputs, and acceptance check

Input：Raw broadband voltage

Output：Channel RMS, channel-frequency power, a quality timeline, line/clipping metrics, and candidate labels

Check：Use the timeline to locate suspicious periods, then verify them in zoomable traces and spectra. Candidate bad channels require confirmation and are not removed automatically.

Next： Carry confirmed bad channels and artifact intervals into separate AP and LFP preprocessing branches.

03

Preprocessing is not a fixed recipe

More preprocessing is not automatically better. Every operation changes the signal, so its purpose, order, and duplication inside the sorter must be explicit. NeuroEphys AI previews a short segment first and compares traces and spectra before parameters are committed to the workflow.

Before you begin

Confirm bad channels and decide whether the target is AP/sorting, LFP, or both.

Operations and purpose

OperationWhat you doPurposeResult
AP / sorting previewChoose high-pass/band-pass, reference, and preview segment.Emphasizes fast action potentials and evaluates sorter input.Shows before/after traces, spectra, and RMS changes.
LFP branch previewChoose low-pass, downsampling, and line-noise handling.Preserves low-frequency population activity and reduces later computation.Creates an independent LFP chain without overwriting the AP branch.
Reference comparisonCompare none, common median, and common average.Reduces common noise while checking whether bad-channel artifacts spread.Displays common components and channel traces before and after referencing.
Commit processing chainSave operation order and parameters after reviewing the preview.Makes sorting, LFP, and reproducibility exports use the same traceable configuration.Stores the processing chain while the source remains read-only.

Parameters

ParameterMeaningDefaultRecommendationEffect of changing it
AP bandPassband used for spike detection or preview.300–6000 HzStart with sorter/domain defaults and adjust only from sampling rate and spectral evidence.A high lower cutoff suppresses broad spikes; a low one retains LFP drift. The upper cutoff must stay below Nyquist.
LFP low-passHighest retained LFP frequency.300 HzUse 150–250 Hz for analyses below 100 Hz; retain a higher cutoff and sampling rate for high-frequency oscillations.Lower cutoffs smooth transients; overly high cutoffs allow spike leakage into LFP.
LFP sampling rateSamples per second retained after LFP filtering.1,000 HzKeep at least twice the highest target frequency; 4–10× margin is common.Too low causes aliasing or poor phase precision; too high increases memory and computation.
ReferenceCommon reference estimate subtracted from each channel.Common medianStart with median for dense recordings; use probe groups and reference design for sparse tetrodes or microwires.Average is more sensitive to outliers; median is robust, but both can remove genuine common activity.
NotchSuppresses 50/60 Hz and optional harmonics.OffEnable only when QC shows narrowband contamination that cannot be corrected at acquisition.Notching changes amplitude and phase around the target and can affect coupling analyses.

Every page control

Before/after traces
Compare filtering and common-median referencing on the same time segment.
Preview stage
Computes a short preview only and never writes over the source recording.
Zoom tools
Inspect polarity, edge effects, clipping, and residual artifacts at sample-level detail.

Recommended sequence

  1. Create separate AP and LFP branches rather than reusing one recipe.
  2. Preview before committing and retain raw, processed, and difference/spectral evidence.
  3. Check whether the sorter already filters, whitens, or corrects drift to avoid duplication.

Common mistakes

  • Whitening both externally and inside Kilosort.
  • Applying a global reference before excluding bad channels.
  • Downsampling before low-pass filtering and causing aliasing.

Inputs, outputs, and acceptance check

Input：Raw voltage and a bad-channel list

Output：AP/sorting and LFP branch previews, a processing chain, and pre-run safeguards

Check：Confirm bad channels before referencing, check Neuropixels inter-sample phase shift, avoid whitening both outside and inside the sorter, and remember that previews never overwrite raw data.

Next： Send the AP branch to Spike sorting and retain the LFP branch for neural analyses.

04

Spike sorting and tool selection

Spike sorting assigns candidate events in multichannel voltage to units. NeuroEphys AI preserves each sorter's native files, parameters, and logs while normalizing shared outputs into units, spike times in seconds, and channel/template descriptions for downstream comparison.

Before you begin

Confirm AP input, probe geometry, bad channels, hardware, output directory, and free disk space.

Operations and purpose

OperationWhat you doPurposeResult
Select a sorterChoose Kilosort4, MountainSort5, or another installed backend.Chooses an algorithm by probe density, hardware, and scientific need, not only availability.Loads that sorter's real settings and shows input preview before a run.
Run selected sorterConfirm data, sorter, parameters, and execution location in the dialog.Prevents expensive runs on the wrong data or configuration.Saves native output, normalized results, versions, settings, logs, and diagnostics.
Inspect run diagnosticsSwitch among drift, spike depth-time, amplitudes, templates, similarity, and logs.Assesses stability across time and probe depth.Produces sorting evidence required before Unit QC.
Compare sortersActivate different results and run unit matching and agreement comparison.Identifies consensus units, splits, merges, and sorter-specific units.Reports agreement on real data and accuracy only when simulation ground truth exists.

Every page control

Sorter table
Lists every candidate, installation status, hardware needs, and suited recording types. Click a row to select.
Parameter preset
Maps recording type to auditable defaults while keeping every value editable.
Run current stage
Runs the selected sorter with current parameters and preserves its full log and result directory.

Recommended sequence

  1. Run a representative segment with defaults to validate input and resources before the full session.
  2. Inspect Kilosort drift, depth-time, amplitude, template, and similarity views.
  3. Preserve native sorter directories; normalized output enables interoperability but does not replace native evidence.

Common mistakes

  • Showing an old result from a different sorter when the selected sorter was not run.
  • Calling cross-sorter agreement on real data accuracy.
  • Comparing only unit counts while ignoring drift, duplicates, amplitudes, and logs.

Inputs, outputs, and acceptance check

Input：High-rate multichannel recording, probe geometry, and sorter parameters

Output：Native sorter files, normalized Unit/spike times in seconds, parameters, logs, and comparison evidence

Check：Verify environment and probe suitability first. Kilosort4 targets dense probes; MountainSort5 is CPU/tetrode-oriented and offers Schemes 1/2/3. After multiple runs, inspect Hungarian matches, agreement, unique units, and consensus units; agreement on real data is not accuracy.

Next： Select an active sorting result and continue to Unit QC; return here when sorter comparison is needed.

05

Unit QC and manual review

Sorter output contains candidate clusters. Unit QC combines firing rate, refractory-period evidence, SNR, waveforms, amplitude over time, and ACGs so the researcher can assign traceable keep, MUA, noise, or uncertain labels. No single threshold replaces review.

Before you begin

Confirm the active sorting result, run log, and drift diagnostics first.

Operations and purpose

OperationWhat you doPurposeResult
Metric scatter and filtersChoose x/y metrics, thresholds, and labels; click a point to inspect a unit.Reveals metric tradeoffs and locates suspicious units.Produces a candidate subset without changing final labels automatically.
Waveforms and templatesInspect mean waveforms and variation on the main and neighboring channels.Assesses temporal stability, spatial plausibility, and noise.Produces unit-level waveform evidence.
ACG and ISIZoom around 0 ms and inspect refractory violations and duplicate peaks.Evaluates contamination, duplicate spikes, and multi-unit mixtures.Produces temporal-structure evidence and refractory metrics.
Manual decisionLabel units good, MUA, noise, or uncertain and add a note.Preserves reviewer decisions and threshold versions for audit and revision.Outputs a traceable review table and downstream unit set.

Parameters

ParameterMeaningDefaultRecommendationEffect of changing it
Firing rateSpikes per second over valid recording time.Report only; no automatic exclusionInterpret with brain region, cell type, duration, and stability.A higher minimum removes sparse units but can discard real low-rate cells.
ISI violation windowWindow used to count refractory-period violations.2 msStart around 1–2 ms and report the exact definition.Larger windows count more violations; values are incomparable without the definition.
SNRWaveform signal magnitude relative to background noise.Report onlyUse one method consistently and inspect waveforms; do not transfer thresholds blindly across tools.Higher thresholds are conservative but penalize real low-amplitude units.
Amplitude stabilityChange in spike amplitude over time.Full-session timelineLook for decay, jumps, or brief presence and compare with drift diagnostics.Overly strict criteria reject real state changes; loose criteria retain drifting or disappearing units.
Contamination thresholdAllowed estimated contamination.Not treated as ground truthIf 10% is shown as a guide, report the estimator and review it with ACG and waveforms.Lower thresholds reduce unit count and increase conservatism, but the estimate is itself uncertain.

Every page control

Unit table
Each row is one candidate unit. Click data points or rows to inspect its metrics.
ISI violation
Measures short refractory-period intervals; the acceptance threshold must be defined by the project.
SNR and waveform
Interpret together with spatial localization, stability, and raw traces; no single metric proves a good unit.

Recommended sequence

  1. Inspect population distributions first, then review boundary and high-impact units.
  2. Thresholds create candidate labels; final decisions should record reviewer, time, and notes.
  3. Recompute QC after changing the active sorter; never reuse metrics from another result.

Common mistakes

  • Declaring a good neuron from one SNR or ISI threshold.
  • Reporting unit count as yield without the screening process.
  • Changing labels manually without preserving an audit record.

Inputs, outputs, and acceptance check

Input：Spike times, waveforms, templates, and duration

Output：Firing rate, ISI violations, SNR, labels, and a review table

Check：Click points to inspect unit metrics. Thresholds are screening rules, not biological truth. Preserve manual decisions.

Next： After selecting downstream units, import behavior and TTL data and establish a common timeline.

06

TTL, behavior, and a common timeline

Behavior computers, cameras, and electrophysiology systems often have independent clocks. The synchronization page fits offset + slope × behavior_time from paired TTL pulses and uses residuals to detect missing pulses, mismatches, or nonlinear drift. Without matching TTLs, NeuroEphys AI can only assume a shared clock and labels that assumption explicitly.

Before you begin

Prepare behavior-event and optional TTL CSV files and identify each column's clock, unit, and event meaning.

Operations and purpose

OperationWhat you doPurposeResult
Import behavior eventsSelect a CSV with time_seconds, trial, condition, and event_type.Builds trial semantics and the event sequence in the behavior clock.Stores the event table and source path in the project.
Import TTL pulsesSelect a CSV containing ephys-clock pulses corresponding to behavior sync pulses.Creates an estimable mapping between device clocks.Shows matched counts, missing pulses, and duplicates.
Fit common timelinePair pulses in order and fit intercept and slope.Corrects initial offset and linear clock drift.Produces ephys-clock seconds, residuals, drift ppm, and a trial table.
Inspect synchronization evidenceInspect residuals over time, event counts, and suspect pairs.Determines whether a linear map is adequate or pairing/segmentation must change.Preserves a synchronization report for every event-aligned analysis.

Parameters

ParameterMeaningDefaultRecommendationEffect of changing it
Behavior time columnColumn containing behavior-device timestamps.time_secondsConvert to seconds while retaining the source column; frame indices require frame rate or per-frame timestamps.A unit mistake creates 1000× or sampling-rate-scale errors.
TTL pairingHow behavior and ephys pulses are paired.Pair one-to-one in orderCheck counts and intervals first; locate missing pulses from interval patterns before pairing.One missing pulse shifts all subsequent order-based pairs.
Clock modelFunction mapping behavior time to ephys time.offset + slope × timeStart linear; use segmented or nonlinear models only for curved residuals or clock jumps.An overly complex model fits pulse jitter; an overly simple model leaves systematic drift.
Residual toleranceMaximum allowed timing error after alignment.Report the observed value without hiding itSet from task timescale and device precision; millisecond spike responses require tighter limits.Loose tolerance retains mismatches; strict tolerance rejects normal TTL jitter.

Every page control

Event count
Confirm that behavior, TTL, and neural events can be matched one-to-one.
Timing residual
Shows paired-event error; a systematic change over time suggests clock drift.
Trial table
Stores conditions, event times, and exclusion flags for downstream analyses.

Recommended sequence

  1. Inspect event/TTL counts and intervals before fitting.
  2. Residual plots must cover the full session, not only a mean error.
  3. Save the mapping, source columns, units, and excluded pairs in the project.

Common mistakes

  • Treating behavior milliseconds as seconds.
  • Forcing row-by-row pairing after a missing pulse.
  • Reporting precise synchronization error without TTL evidence.

Inputs, outputs, and acceptance check

Input：Spike times in seconds, TTLs, behavioral events, and video frames

Output：Common time base, matching residuals, and a trial table

Check：Check event counts, order, residuals, drift, dropped frames, and duplicate pulses.

Next： Use the synchronized trial table to inspect behavior before event-aligned neural analysis.

07

Behavior quality precedes neural interpretation

Behavior analysis first asks whether the experiment ran as designed: trial counts per condition, choice balance, reaction times, missing values, and exclusions. Neural responses and machine-learning labels become interpretable only after behavior structure is reliable.

Before you begin

The synchronization page has produced a common timeline and trial table with start, event, and end definitions.

Every page control

Condition counts
Check class balance and whether every condition has enough trials.
Reaction time

Recommended sequence

  1. Generate and save behavior figures before neural figures.
  2. Make every plotted point inspectable for stimulus level, count, and source trials.

08

Neural activity analysis workbench

The neural page is not a single fixed PSTH. It contains independently runnable event-response, spike-train, LFP spectral/time-frequency, and spike-field coupling analyses. Every sub-analysis has its own inputs, settings, figures, and tables; before execution it shows an input preview, never fabricated results.

Before you begin

Confirm active units, synchronized events, and valid trials; LFP and coupling also require voltage data.

Operations and purpose

09

From figures to statistical evidence

Statistics begins with the sampling unit, not a test name. Trials are nested in sessions and units in animals; pairing, repeated measures, and hierarchy determine valid comparisons. The page reports effects, confidence intervals, and multiplicity handling rather than treating p-values as the only result.

Before you begin

Prepare unsmoothed trial-level metrics, conditions, unit/session/animal IDs, and exclusions.

Operations and purpose

OperationWhat you doPurposeResult
Design checkSpecify sampling unit, pairing, hierarchy, and primary comparison.Prevents pseudoreplication and treating nested samples as independent.Produces an auditable statistical-design summary.
Parametric/nonparametric comparisonChoose t/Welch/ANOVA or Wilcoxon/Mann–Whitney/Kruskal from the design.Compares conditions under explicit assumptions and data structure.Outputs statistic, p-value, effect, confidence interval, and sample size.
Permutation/bootstrapShuffle labels or resample at the legal exchangeability unit.Builds null distributions or intervals with fewer distributional assumptions.Saves random seed, repetitions, and the complete chance distribution.
Mixed-effects modelSet fixed effects and animal/session/unit random effects.Handles hierarchical, missing, and unbalanced repeated measures.Outputs formula, coefficients, intervals, convergence, and diagnostics.

  2. Report raw observations, effect, confidence interval, p-value, and correction together.

Common mistakes

  • Treating 300 units from three animals as n=300 independent observations.
  • Testing every PSTH bin without multiplicity control.
  • Reporting only p-values without direction, magnitude, or uncertainty.

Machine learning, clustering, and leakage

Machine Learning converts neural activity to a trial-by-feature matrix and places preprocessing, feature selection, and the model inside cross-validation. The goal is not the highest score but a leakage-free evaluation against a shuffle baseline with session/animal generalization.

Before you begin

Define prediction target, feature window, grouping variable, class balance, and minimum sample size.

Operations and purpose

OperationWhat you doPurposeResult
ClassificationChoose Logistic regression, SVM, Random forest, XGBoost, or another model.Predicts discrete stimulus, choice, or outcome labels.Outputs balanced accuracy, F1, AUC, confusion matrix, and fold scores.
RegressionChoose linear, Ridge, random-forest, or another regressor.Predicts position, speed, reaction time, or continuous stimulus values.Outputs R², MAE, predicted-versus-observed plots, and residuals.
Time-resolved decodingRepeat the complete cross-validation inside sliding time bins.Estimates how predictive information changes around an event.Outputs a time course, chance distribution, and multiplicity results.

  • Selecting units on all data before cross-validation.
  • Showing only the best model after undisclosed model comparison.
